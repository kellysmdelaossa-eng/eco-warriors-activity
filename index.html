01 · Pedagogical Context & Rationale

Why Gamification Unlocks Speaking in 7th Grade

At IED Santa Ana in Barbosa, Santander, 7th-grade learners (level A2) often hesitate to speak English aloud when classes center on silent grammar drills. Grounded in Gamification and Game-Based Learning, this mission replaces rote translation with meaningful quests, immediate feedback, and team badges.

Following Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, and relatedness) and Krashen’s Affective Filter Hypothesis, playful team missions lower speaking anxiety while aligning with Colombia’s MEN Suggested Curriculum & DBA for environmental citizenship.

02 · Communicative Learning Objective

By the end of this mission, learners will orally negotiate, propose, and present three actionable eco-solutions to protect IED Santa Ana’s green spaces—using environmental vocabulary and modal verbs (should, must, can) with clarity and collaborative peer support.

Skill: A2 Speaking & Interaction Grammar: should · must · can Setting: Barbosa Campus Green Zones

03 · Instructions for Learners

How Your Squad Completes the Mission

Step 1 · Pre-Class Prep Earn Your Recruit Badge Listen to the 8 eco-terms and pass the 5-question mission briefing before entering class (+150 XP).
Step 2 · Pick a Role Build a Safe 4-Person Squad Choose a flexible role (Scout, Strategist, Time Keeper, or Reporter) so every classmate speaks safely.
Step 3 · Green Stations Solve Campus Eco-Dilemmas Visit Santa Ana’s 4 green stations, run the 3-minute speaking sprint, and discuss ideas with modals.
Step 4 · Pitch & Log Record & Export Evidence Evaluate your speaking with the formative rubric and download your squad’s 3-proposal evidence sheet.
Golden Rule: Errors help us grow—never laugh at a teammate trying English!

    06 · Inclusive Pedagogy & Universal Design for Learning

    UDL Toolkit & Visual Phrase Scaffolding Cards

    Designed so every 7th-grade student at IED Santa Ana—including shy speakers, auditory/visual learners, and those needing extra linguistic scaffolding—can participate and shine without fear.

    UDL Principle 1

    Multiple Means of Engagement

    Gamified quests, choice of campus green stations, and mistake-friendly small squads lower Krashen’s affective filter and nurture autonomy.

    UDL Principle 2

    Multiple Means of Representation

    Visual icons, color-coded modal frames, word-stress marks, spoken audio playback, and optional L1 Spanish cues clarify A2 language demands.

    UDL Principle 3

    Multiple Means of Expression

    Students choose supportive peer roles, rehearse with the Speak-Safe Sentence Forge, and co-present in pairs instead of facing solo spotlighting.
